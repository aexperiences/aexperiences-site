The Store

The Store Is the Product

Not the apps. The store. One place where everything we make is live, priced, and enterable — no demo, no sales call, no download.
Anthony EspositoAugust 9, 20262 min read

Ask a software company what they sell and they'll name an app. Ask us and the answer is the store.

The AE App Shop works like a real shop. Two doors: one for your business, one for you. Tap anything on any shelf and you walk straight into the real thing — no demo, no sales call, no download, and for most of the consumer shelf, no account just to look around. Fourteen complete business operating systems. Thirty-one consumer apps. One place.

Software stores show you screenshots. Ours lets you in.

Why build it this way? Because every alternative is a toll booth between you and the truth. A demo call exists so a salesperson can control what you see. A screenshot gallery exists because the product can't survive a stranger clicking around unsupervised. We removed both. If a thing is on the shelf marked live, a stranger can use it today. If it isn't ready, it says so plainly and there's nothing to buy. That rule is enforced in the catalog itself — an entry can't claim more than it is.

The store is also how a small family business can sell into fourteen industries at once. There are no landing pages, no per-product funnels, no brochure sites to maintain. One catalog, one price record, one front door. When something new ships — and something ships most days — it lands on a shelf in the same store, and the store gets a little better. That compounding is the product. An individual app is a thing we made; the store is the thing we're building.

Entry prices for the business systems run $99 to $650 a month depending on the vertical, flat, with the whole team included. The consumer shelf is mostly free to try. Both doors are open right now.
