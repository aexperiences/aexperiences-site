The Day We Went From Nine Industries to Fourteen

Home care, field service, tattoo, dental, photo and video — five complete operating systems in one working day. Here's the honest version of how that's possible.
Anthony EspositoAugust 16, 20263 min read

At the end of July, the business side of the AE App Shop grew by five industries in one working day. Home care got Stay@Home OS. Field service got Toolbelt OS. Tattoo studios got Sleeves OS. Dental practices got Smiley OS. Photo and video studios got Moments OS. We opened the morning selling operating systems to nine industries and closed the day selling to fourteen. All fourteen are live in the store right now — you can walk in and open any of them before you finish reading this.

It wasn't a fast day of building. It was a slow twenty-one years, cashed in.

I want to be straight about what that day was, because on its face it sounds like a stunt — and a stunt is exactly what it wasn't. Five industries in a day sounds like software slapped together. So let me tell you what actually made it possible, because the answer isn't in the software.

Every business day has the same bones. Something comes in the door — a lead, a patient, a booking, a work order. It gets scheduled. Somebody does the work. Money gets asked for and collected. Paper proves it all happened. I have spent twenty-one years inside that loop: building targeting models for newspapers, running market studies for national media groups, managing national accounts, running operations in senior living. Different industries every time. Same bones every time.

By the time the ninth operating system shipped, that spine wasn't a theory I was hoping would hold. It had already held — for real estate, for engineering firms, for theaters, for a kids' gym. A dental practice and a tattoo studio don't believe they're alike, but look at their day: chairs, appointments, deposits, before-and-after photos, consent forms. What kept the day honest was respecting the parts that are truly different. Home care runs on visits and care plans. Field service runs on trucks, jobs, and quotes out of a van. A photo studio lives or dies on delivering the goods after the shoot. Each of the five got its own rooms, named the way that industry actually talks.

That's what the day actually took: knowing each industry's day well enough to name its rooms right. The market research decides which doors we build — that part of how we work hasn't changed since the beginning. Ideas come in from family and friends, the research says yes or no, and when it says yes, we build the whole thing, not a teaser.

As for how the building itself moves that fast — that's the one door I keep closed. Every builder keeps one, and that's mine.

The speed isn't the claim that matters anyway. This is: all fourteen are live, priced, and enterable by a stranger with no demo, no sales call, and no download. Flat monthly, your whole team included, no per-seat fees. If you run a shop in one of those fourteen industries, the door with your name on it is already open.
