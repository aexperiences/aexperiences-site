Why There's No Per-Seat Fee

Hire your tenth tech without paying for the privilege. One flat monthly, the whole team included, and you keep 100% of your revenue.
Anthony EspositoJuly 29, 20262 min read

Industry software has a habit it hopes you won't examine: it charges you for growing. Another seat license when you hire. A per-ticket toll when your show sells. A per-deal cut when your agent closes. The better your business does, the bigger the vendor's slice — and none of that money bought you anything new.

Every operating system in our store prices the other way. One flat monthly for the whole operation. Every seat included — hire your tenth tech, your fifth agent, your new front desk, and the bill doesn't move. No per-ticket fees, no per-deal fees, no percentage of anything. You keep 100% of your revenue, and hosting is included. If you ever leave, your data leaves with you.

A vendor that taxes your growth is a partner only when you're small.

Entry prices run $99 to $650 a month depending on the vertical — a photo studio doesn't pay dental-practice money — and every price is published on the store next to the product it belongs to. No quote forms, no "contact sales," no number that changes depending on who's asking. The same page shows all three tiers of every system, and you can walk into the top tier and use it before anyone talks to you.

There's one real choice to make, and it isn't a feature matrix. Every system comes in two editions: run it under our name, or run it under yours — same software, white-labeled to your brand. Whose name is on it. That's the decision.

We sell it this way because of what we're actually selling — which isn't a feature list. It's worth: the system runs your whole operation, it gets better every week, and it never punishes you for succeeding. Go price your current stack against that, and then come walk around the store.
