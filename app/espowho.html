ESPOwho

Twenty questions, out loud

Open ESPOwho → Free

What it is

Think of somebody and it guesses who. Mario, Pikachu, Bluey, Michael Jordan, a shark. It asks yes-or-no questions until it works out who you were thinking of — or flip it round, and it picks somebody in secret while your kid does the asking.

ESPOwho runs in your browser. There is nothing to download and no account needed — the link above opens the real software, not a preview or a video of it.

It reads everything out loud

Not just the questions. The buttons, the menus, the worlds you can pick, the name at the end. A child who cannot read a word yet can open this and play the whole thing on their own.

Every word lights up as it is spoken, so a kid follows along and starts matching the sound to the shape without anybody making a lesson out of it. And they can answer out loud too — yeah, nope, nah, dunno, I don't know all land, and they can say a name instead of hunting for the picture. The buttons never go away, because a loud kitchen mishears.

It does not ask the same questions twice

The questions are not a list somebody typed out. The game builds them from what it knows about the characters, which is why there are twelve hundred of them, and it remembers which ones it has already used. Round four does not open with round one's question.

Three sizes, one game

Little, Middle and Big Brain, so a six-year-old and a twelve-year-old can play the same game without one of them being bored and the other being lost. Nine worlds: video games, Pokemon, cartoons and movies, superheroes, sports legends, animals, dinosaurs, space and nature, and everyday things.

Playing against each other

One of you starts a game and gets a four-letter code. Read it to the other person, they type it in, and you take turns. Nobody has to be sitting there at the same time — you answer when you pick the tablet up. The codes leave out the letters that get misheard, because a child has to be able to read one down a phone.
