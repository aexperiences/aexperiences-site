The notebook

Plain-English notes. No jargon for its own sake.

Nose, palate, finish

Three moments, in order. The nose is what you smell before you sip; the palate is what lands on the tongue; the finish is what stays after you swallow. Writing all three is what turns a bottle into a memory.

Age statements

An age statement names the YOUNGEST whiskey in the bottle. NAS means no age stated — which is not automatically worse, just unstated.

What the cask does

Most of the colour and a large share of the flavour come from the wood. Ex-bourbon casks tend toward vanilla and caramel; sherry casks toward dried fruit and spice. A finish is a second cask at the end.

Proof and ABV

ABV is alcohol by volume. US proof is exactly double the ABV — 43% ABV is 86 proof. Cask strength means it was not cut with water.

A splash of water

A few drops can open a high-proof pour, releasing aromas the alcohol was masking. Try it neat first, then add water — you cannot take it back out.

Stirred or shaken

Stir anything that is all spirit — Old Fashioned, Manhattan, Boulevardier. It stays clear and silky. Shake when there is citrus, egg or dairy, because you need aeration and a fast chill. Shaking an all-spirit drink just makes it cloudy and waters it down.

Dilution is an ingredient

A properly stirred cocktail picks up roughly a fifth to a quarter of its volume in water. That is not a mistake, it is the recipe — it is what turns hot spirit into something you can drink. Ice size controls how fast it happens.

Two templates cover most of it

Spirit, sugar, bitters, water — that is an Old Fashioned, and a surprising number of classics are variations on it. Spirit, citrus, sweetener is the sour, which gives you the Whiskey Sour, the Gold Rush and the Paper Plane. Learn the two shapes and most recipes stop being memorisation.

Vermouth is wine

It oxidises like wine, because it is wine. Refrigerate it once opened and use it within a few weeks. A Manhattan that tastes tired is almost always tired vermouth, not tired whiskey.

Which bottle to mix

High-proof rye or bourbon holds its shape through ice and modifiers. A delicate older single malt mostly disappears behind vermouth and bitters — which is an expensive way to make a Manhattan. Save the subtle bottles for the Pour side.
